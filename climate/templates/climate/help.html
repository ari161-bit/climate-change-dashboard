Getting started

  1. Sign in (or create an Analyst account from the login page).
  2. The Dashboard opens on the warming stripes and headline numbers. Everything below responds to the year range you choose.
  3. Use the moon/sun button in the header to switch between light and dark themes. Your choice is remembered.
  4. Stuck or found something odd? Use Support to message the agency team; replies appear on the same page.

Page by page

Dashboard

Five sections: The warming signal (temperature, CO2, anomalies, projection), Drivers (emissions by country), Impacts (sea level, glaciers, disasters, forests), Regions and cities and Relationships (cross-dataset correlation). Charts can be zoomed by dragging, and every chart has a menu to export an image or CSV.

Data explorer

Browse every dataset in the data lake, switch the column or group (for example a country or a city) and see its source, checksum and data-quality figures.

Live

Real-time temperature, air quality and CO2 for twelve cities, refreshed every minute, set against each city's ten-year mean from the historical data.

Satellite

Daily NASA imagery on an interactive map: true colour, aerosols, vegetation, land temperature and snow.

Trends

The trend analyzer fits a trend to any country or IMF region for forest and carbon indicators or climate-related disasters. You get the recent 10-year average, how well the trend fits (R², RMSE), the years that depart from it (outliers) and a projection to 2030-2050 with a 95% band.

Models

The four analytical models, their accuracy and the history of each retraining run.

Tutorials

Tutorial 1: measure how quickly a period warmed

  1. On the Dashboard, press Since 1980.
  2. Read Warming rate in the headline cards, and the decade chart for the pattern.
  3. Compare the line of months above +1 °C: it shows how common once-rare warmth has become.

Tutorial 1b: project a region's forest carbon

  1. Open Trends. The defaults show Carbon stocks in forests for Advanced Economies.
  2. Change the region to Africa or a single country and the horizon to 2050.
  3. Compare R² and the outlier list: a low R² or many outliers means the trend line is a poor summary of that series.

Tutorial 2: check whether a city is warming faster than the planet

  1. Scroll to Regions and cities and read the ranking.
  2. Compare a city's trend with the global rate in the cards above.
  3. Open the Data explorer, choose City temperatures, pick the city and the hottest_day column to see how the hottest day of each year has changed.

Tutorial 3: act on a live alert

  1. Open Live. Alerts appear at the top when a city is above a threshold.
  2. Open Satellite with the Aerosol layer to see whether haze or smoke explains a poor air-quality reading.
  3. If you think a threshold should change, message the team via Support; administrators change thresholds under Admin → Alerts, data & inbox.

Tutorial 4 (administrators): add your own dataset

  1. Open Admin → Alerts, data & inbox and find Upload a dataset.
  2. Choose a CSV, JSON or XLS file that has a year (or date) column and a numeric column. Dates are averaged to yearly values.
  3. Pick who may see it: analysts as well, or administrators only. It then appears under Uploaded in the Data explorer.

How alerts work

Each alert compares a current value with a threshold. It is normal below the threshold, an alert above it and critical at 1.5× the threshold (live alerts use 1.15× for heat and 2× for particulates). Climate alerts are recalculated whenever the dashboard loads; live alerts are recalculated on every live refresh. Alerts are shown in the portal; the system does not send email or text messages.

Frequently asked questions

What is a temperature "anomaly"?The difference between a temperature and the 1951-1980 average for the same place and time of year. +1.2 °C means 1.2 °C warmer than that baseline. Anomalies are used because they are comparable across places and seasons.
Is the 2050 projection a forecast?No. It extends the statistical trend of recent decades and shows its uncertainty band. It contains no emissions scenarios or climate physics, so treat it as an illustration of the current trajectory.
Why does the correlation table show strong links between almost everything?Many climate series rise together over time, which produces high correlations whatever the cause. A strong value says the series move together, not that one causes the other.
Why do some charts have gaps or stop earlier than others?Each source starts and ends on its own schedule (CO2 from 1958, disasters from 1970, sea level to about 2020). Short gaps in continuous series are interpolated; longer ones are left blank.
How fresh is the data?Temperature and CO2 are published monthly and loaded by the ingestion command. The Live page is real-time, cached for up to five minutes.
Where does my feedback go, and is it private?It is stored encrypted and is visible only to you and to administrators.
Why was I signed out or shown "form expired"?Sessions last eight hours, and a form that was open in another tab before you signed in becomes invalid. Reload the page and try again.

Glossary

ppm
Parts per million: molecules of CO2 per million molecules of air.
Reanalysis (MERRA-2)
A physically consistent reconstruction of past weather that blends observations with a model; NASA POWER uses it for the city records.
RMSE
Root mean squared error: the typical size of a model's prediction error, in the data's own units.
z-score
How many standard deviations a value lies from a reference mean.
Mass balance
Net gain or loss of glacier ice, in metres of water equivalent.
PM2.5
Fine particles under 2.5 micrometres across that reach deep into the lungs.
